## 0. The connective node: Joseph "Junior" Hicks

Hicks is the single person linking the 1960s–70s Uintah Basin flap to the Sherman
story. This is the most important structural fact in the provenance chain and is
rarely stated plainly.

- 1974: Hicks is the field data collector behind Salisbury's *The Utah UFO Display*. `[PRIMARY-CITED]`
- June 1996: Deseret News reporter Zack Van Eyck, assigned a UFO story, calls Hicks
  as the regional expert. **Hicks tells Van Eyck to call Gwen Sherman at the bank
  where she worked.** Van Eyck phones Gwen within minutes. `[SECONDARY]`

**Chain:** Hicks (local UFO clearinghouse, ~30 years of accumulated basin lore) →
Van Eyck → Gwen Sherman → print. The Shermans did not approach the press
independently; they were surfaced by the region's pre-existing UFO chronicler.

### 1.3 Individual incident catalog

**The wolf / large canid and the .357**
- **Claimed:** A wolf-like animal ~3× normal size seized a calf by the snout through
  the corral bars. Terry ("Tom Gorman") and his father beat it with sticks; no release.
  Gorman fired a .357 Magnum at point-blank range — no effect. A second round made it
  release the calf, but it stood as if nothing happened. Two more rounds: it backed off,
  no distress, **no blood**. Framing: Gorman is an experienced marksman; five slugs
  would drop an elk.
- **Source:** Kelleher & Knapp (2005), Ch. 1. Every retelling found uses the **"Gorman"
  pseudonym** — a textual fingerprint proving derivation from the book rather than the
  newspaper. `[BOOK-ONLY]`
- **Firsthand?** Book narrates Terry Sherman's account; authors not present. Secondhand
  at minimum, reported to NIDS retrospectively.
- **Physical evidence:** None. No carcass, no recovered rounds, no photographs, no
  veterinary record.
- **Variant:** some retellings have the family's *car* attacked by "several huge wolves"
  — an internal inconsistency in secondary accounts.

**The three dogs**
- **Claimed:** ~May 1996. Terry Sherman outside with three dogs; a **blue orb** darts
  around the field. He urges the dogs after it. The orb leads them into thick brush.
  He hears **three yelps**. Next morning he finds **three round, greasy lumps with
  scorched spots**.
- **Source:** Book (2005) for the full narrative `[BOOK-ONLY]`; but the *kernel* —
  "dog-killing balls of light" — is attested to the Shermans in the *Deseret News*,
  April 27, 1997 `[PRIMARY-CITED]`. **Not a 2005 invention.**
- **Firsthand?** Terry Sherman only. No second witness named.
- **Physical evidence:** None. No samples, no soil analysis, no photographs.

**Voices in a foreign language overhead**
- **Claimed:** Terry Sherman hears **male voices speaking an unfamiliar language,
  roughly 25 feet above him**.
- **Source:** `[BOOK-ONLY]` / `[SECONDARY]`. Not found in 1996 article summaries.
- **Firsthand?** Terry Sherman alone. No corroborating witness. No recording.

**Invisible object crushing brush / destructive magnetic fields**
- **Claimed:** An unseen mass flattening brush; NIDS-era framing describes "invisible
  objects emitting destructive magnetic fields."
- **Source:** `[BOOK-ONLY]` and NIDS-era summaries.
- **Physical evidence:** No magnetometer traces, calibration records, or instrument
  logs ever published.

**The bull in the trailer**
- **Claimed:** After bulls went missing, Terry found animals crammed into a disused
  trailer in a hypnotized/stupefied state — in some tellings inside a container they
  could not physically have entered unaided.
- **Source:** `[BOOK-ONLY]` / `[SECONDARY]`. **Physical evidence:** None. No photographs.

**Blue orbs** — attested in the book and, as "balls of light," in the April 1997

**Plants / soil** — the June 1996 article does reference **circles of flattened grass

## 2. NIDS Era (1996–2004)

### 2.1 Institutional facts

- NIDS founded **1995** by Robert T. Bigelow, Las Vegas; operated 1995–2004;
  **officially shut down October 20, 2004**. `[SECONDARY]`
- Personnel of record: **Col. John B. Alexander** (director of scientific liaison),
  **Colm A. Kelleher** (deputy administrator / project manager), **Eric Davis**,
  **Jacques Vallée**.
- Bigelow purchased the ranch **September 1996** for **~$200,000** (accounts range
  $200k–$230k), three months after the Van Eyck article. Van Eyck, "Millionaire leads
  quest for UFO data," *Deseret News*, **Oct 20, 1996** `[PRIMARY-CITED]`; corroborated
  by *Las Vegas Sun*, **Oct 23, 1996** `[PRIMARY-CITED]`

### 2.2 The NDA — documented contemporaneously

*Deseret News*, **Aug 10, 1998**, "Private UFO study takes a public turn"
<https://www.deseret.com/1998/8/10/19395824/private-ufo-study-takes-a-public-turn/> `[PRIMARY-CITED]`

- **As a condition of purchase, the Shermans agreed to keep quiet about what they witnessed.**
- Terry Sherman was retained as **ranch manager**.

**The pivotal chain-of-custody fact of the NIDS era:** after September 1996 the sole
continuous on-site observer is financially and contractually tied to the investigating
organization, under a confidentiality condition.

### 2.3 THE MAJOR CONTRADICTION — NIDS's own 1997 negative

*Deseret News*, **April 27, 1997**, "No UFOs or ETs have dropped in at spooky ranch" `[PRIMARY-CITED]`

> Researchers "have yet to see the flying craft, cattle mutilations and dog-killing
> 'balls of light'" the Shermans described.
>
> **John Alexander, on the record: "There have been some minor observations, but
> nothing you would stand behind."**

**This directly conflicts with the book's chronology.** The book places the **March 10,
1997 calf mutilation** — a NIDS-attended event — roughly **seven weeks before** this
article, in which NIDS's own scientific liaison director says they have not seen cattle
mutilations and have nothing they would stand behind. Both cannot be straightforwardly true.

### 2.4 The March 10, 1997 calf

- **Claimed:** The ranch manager and his wife (= the Shermans) tagged an **84-pound
  calf**. Returning **45 minutes later**, in broad daylight, they found the body cavity
  empty — most body weight gone including muscle mass, all internal organs, all blood.
  NIDS staff on scene **within five hours**. Potential eyewitnesses a few hundred yards
  away throughout.
- **Chain:** Shermans (firsthand discovery) → NIDS (secondhand, arrived hours later) →
  Kelleher & Knapp 2005. `[BOOK-ONLY]` for narrative detail.
- **Physical evidence:** No published necropsy report, photographs, lab results, or
  chain-of-custody documentation for **this specific calf** has ever been released.

### 2.5 The "portal / creature" — August 28, 1997

- **Claimed:** ~2 AM. A dull white light appears, grows, resembles a portal. Through
  night-vision binoculars it appears as a **"3-D tunnel"**; a large muscular **humanoid
  with no facial features** drags itself through, "almost using its elbows," then
  disappears into the darkness.
- **NAMING CONTRADICTION — documented:** Kelleher & Knapp (2005) name the observers
  **"Jim" and "Mike."** Jacques Vallée, in *Forbidden Science: Volume Four*, names them
  as **Chad Deetken and Terry Sherman**. `[SECONDARY, well-documented]`
- **Consequence:** one of the two observers is **Terry Sherman himself** — the
  originating claimant and paid ranch manager — not an independent NIDS scientist. The
  common "NIDS scientists saw a creature come through a portal" framing **overstates
  witness independence**.
- **Physical evidence:** None. No photograph, no video, no night-vision recording,
  **despite the site being under NIDS surveillance instrumentation at the time.**

### 2.6 The "precognitive sentient phenomenon"

- **Attribution correction:** this concept belongs to **Col. John B. Alexander**, not
  Kelleher. Formulation: a "pre-cognitive sentient intelligence" that anticipated what
  the team would do and disabled their equipment accordingly.
- **Function:** an unfalsifiable auxiliary hypothesis explaining why instrumentation
  produced nothing. Provenance-relevant because it is the stated in-house reason for
  the absence of physical evidence.

### 2.7 Did NIDS publish peer-reviewed data? Where did the data go?

- **Kelleher's described methodology:** field necropsies with veterinarians; tissue
  samples to accredited labs; he reported **two distinct patterns of tissue removal**
  across dozens of cases. `[SECONDARY]`
- NIDS circulated a paper linking cattle mutilations to covert **TSE ("mad cow")
  surveillance** — a self-published institutional white paper, **not peer-reviewed**.
- `[UNRESOLVED]` No peer-reviewed NIDS publication presenting ranch data could be confirmed.
- **No final NIDS report was ever published.** Knapp and Kelleher later discuss an
  "unpublicized NIDS report" — framing that is itself an admission it was never released.
- **Stated reason for shutdown (Kelleher):** phenomena "seemed to dramatically decrease
  starting in 2000"; too few cases merited scrutiny; a lack of physical-trace cases
  warranting analysis.
- **Disposition of the data:** not publicly established. It did not enter the
  peer-reviewed literature. Portions appear carried forward into BAASS/AAWSAP by the
  same personnel.

## 3. *Hunt for the Skinwalker* (2005)

- **Authors:** Colm A. Kelleher, PhD (biochemist; NIDS deputy administrator — an
  investigator writing up his own institution's work) and **George Knapp** (KLAS-TV
  investigative journalist).
- **Publisher:** Paraview Pocket Books / Simon & Schuster, ISBN 9781416505211. `[PRIMARY-CITED]`
- **Evidentiary standard:**
  - **Pseudonymous principals.** The Shermans appear as **"Tom and Ellen Gorman."**
    NIDS witnesses as **"Jim" and "Mike."** Real names were later supplied by third
    parties (Vallée) and the press — not by the book.
  - Narrative-reconstructive: scenes with dialogue, including single-witness events and
    events years prior to writing.
  - **No appendix of instrument data, lab reports, necropsy documentation, photographs
    of key incidents, or raw logs.** The most dramatic claims — wolf, dogs,
    portal/creature — carry **no physical evidence whatsoever, by the book's own account.**
- **Critical response:**
  - **Robert Sheaffer**, *Skeptical Inquirer* (May 2020): the book is "full of spooky
    stories and absolutely no empirical evidence to back any of them up"; the
    "'phenomenon' at Skinwalker is almost certainly illusory." His **parsimonious
    explanation**: the family invented the story "prior to selling it to the gullible
    Bigelow." `[SECONDARY]`
  - **Barry Greenwood**, *Journal of Scientific Exploration* (2023): criticized the $22M
    Lacatski program, emphasized "the lack of any documentary evidence from the ranch
    after many decades of exploration," and characterized Skinwalker as "always in the
    business of selling belief and hope." `[SECONDARY]`
  - **Jacques Vallée**, *Forbidden Science Vol. 4*: contemporaneous diary material that
    **de-anonymizes and partially contradicts** the book's portal account. The most
    valuable independent cross-check on the book that exists.

### 4.1 Established facts

  two colleagues (commonly Ted Stevens and Daniel Inouye).
- **DIA awarded the contract in 2008** to **Bigelow Aerospace Advanced Space Studies
  (BAASS)**. Program: **Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program (AAWSAP)**,
  running **2008–2012**.
- **DIA program manager: Dr. James T. Lacatski.** BAASS leadership included **Colm
  Kelleher** — the same principal as the NIDS era.
- **Did it study the ranch? Yes, by contract design.** BAASS's **proposal itself
  specified** research at BAASS headquarters in Las Vegas **and at Skinwalker Ranch**.
  The "ranch was central to AAWSAP" claim rests on contract scope, not later
  reinterpretation. `[SECONDARY, well-attested]`
- The "**primary focus**" phrasing derives principally from **Lacatski, Kelleher &
  Knapp's own later book** *Skinwalkers at the Pentagon* (2021) — the same author group. `[BOOK-ONLY]`
- Output: **100+ technical reports** to DIA; **~38 papers** collected by BAASS (the DIRDs).

### 4.2 The "human effects" material

- The relevant document is **"Anomalous Acute and Subacute Field Effects on Human

  <https://ufotrail.blogspot.com/2022/04/the-ufo-injury-study-that-wasnt.html>;

**AAWSAP ≠ AATIP.** AAWSAP is the 2008–2012 DIA-contracted BAASS program that included
the ranch. AATIP is the label later applied to a narrower/successor effort associated
with Luis Elizondo. Conflation is the source of most public confusion about whether
"the Pentagon studied Skinwalker Ranch." **The ranch work sits under AAWSAP/BAASS.**

## 6. Ute / Navajo Context — the name is misattributed

### 6.1 The core category error

- **"Skinwalker" is Navajo (Diné): *yee naaldlooshii*,** roughly "by means of it, he
  goes on all fours." A specific, culturally embedded concept within Diné spiritual and
  social systems — not a generic shapeshifter.
- **The ranch is in the Uintah Basin, on/adjacent to the Ute Indian Tribe of the Uintah
  and Ouray Reservation — not Navajo Nation**, which is ~350 miles south.
- The name "Skinwalker Ranch" is **not** what the Shermans called it and not what the
  June 1996 article called it (the "Sherman ranch"). **The name is a later
  marketing/publishing overlay, popularized by the 2005 book title.**

### 6.2 The "Ute curse" claim

- The standard narrative asserts a longstanding Navajo–Ute conflict leaving a curse in
  contested territory.
- **Ute cultural authority pushback:** **Betsy Chapoose**, a Ute tribal cultural-rights
  figure, is reported to have said **she personally never heard of a Navajo curse on
  that specific ranch parcel.** `[SECONDARY — high-value verification target]`
- Counterpoint: some Ute oral tradition does describe the area as spiritually fraught.
  **Tribal representatives and historians caution the curse narrative is debated and not
  uniformly attested among Ute people.**

### 6.3 Diné scholarly objection

- **Adrienne Keene** (Navajo/Cherokee scholar, *Native Appropriations*) has critiqued
  outsider appropriation, arguing skinwalker stories have "context, roots, and reality"
  and should not be lifted into outsider entertainment.
- General position in the sources: the legend is rarely discussed with outsiders at all;
  commercialization treats sacred narrative as commodity entertainment. Media producers
  "stitch together Ute and Navajo histories, local anecdotes, UFO lore and government
  research," privileging spectacle over cultural accuracy.
- `[UNRESOLVED]` No formal statement from the **Navajo Nation** or **Ute Indian Tribe
  Business Committee** objecting to the ranch's name was located.

## Structural provenance findings

1. **Every dramatic Sherman-era claim rests on a single family, and after Sept 1996 on
   a single paid employee of the investigating body.** No independent witness to the
   wolf, the dogs, the voices, the bull, or the invisible object has ever been named.
2. **Zero physical evidence survives from the Sherman era.** No carcass, no recovered
   bullet, no tissue sample, no soil analysis, no photograph, no video, no instrument
   log has been published for any signature incident — **including incidents that
   occurred while the property was under NIDS surveillance instrumentation.**
3. **No peer-reviewed publication and no final report emerged from eight years and an
   eight-figure private research program.**
4. **The same three principals recur across all four eras** — Kelleher, Knapp, and
   Bigelow, joined by Lacatski — spanning NIDS (1996–2004), the book (2005),
   AAWSAP/BAASS (2008–2012), and *Skinwalkers at the Pentagon* (2021). **Claims that
   look like independent corroboration across decades frequently trace to the same
   small author group.**
5. **The property's famous name is an editorial/commercial overlay** applying a Navajo
   concept to Ute land, popularized by the 2005 book title.
