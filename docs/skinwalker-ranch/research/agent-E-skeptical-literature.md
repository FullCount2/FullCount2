## 1. The AARO / Kirkpatrick Historical Record Report (March 2024)

*Report on the Historical Record of U.S. Government Involvement with UAP, Volume 1*,
released 8 March 2024. Covers all official USG UAP effort 1945 – October 2023.

**On AAWSAP/Skinwalker — the key structural finding [B/C]:** the report states that while
AAWSAP's official purpose was research into twelve scientific/technical areas, "the
contractor team and at least one supportive government program manager also conducted UAP
and paranormal research at a private sector property."

> **This is the most important detail, and it cuts both ways.** The report reportedly
> **does not name Skinwalker Ranch** — it uses "a private sector property." The
> identification is an inference (universally made, and almost certainly correct, since
> BAASS/Bigelow owned it). But **AARO declined to name it**, and anyone quoting AARO as
> "AARO says Skinwalker Ranch" is adding a step. **Verify exact wording and page number.**

**Direct quotes on evidence [A]:**
- "To date, AARO has found no verifiable evidence for claims that the U.S. government and
  private companies have access to or have been reverse-engineering extraterrestrial
  technology."
- AARO assessed that "all of the named and described alleged hidden UAP
  reverse-engineering programs provided by interviewees either do not exist; are
  misidentified authentic, highly sensitive national security programs that are not
  related to extraterrestrial technology exploitation; or resolve to an unwarranted and
  disestablished program."
- On AAWSAP/AATIP-affiliated claimants: "They have not provided any empirical evidence of
  their claims to AARO." **[B]**

**Strength: strong but narrow.** AARO is authoritative on *what the government did and did
not conclude.* It is **not** a scientific investigation of the ranch. It does not say
"nothing happens at Skinwalker Ranch"; it says the programme drifted into paranormal
research without operational utility and that claimants produced no empirical evidence.
**Do not overstate it.**

**Rebuttal [B]:** Christopher Mellon (former Deputy Assistant Secretary of Defense for
Intelligence) and Robert Powell (SCU) both publicly flagged **factual errors and sourcing
problems**. Lacatski disputes Kirkpatrick's account of Kona Blue. **This is real,
substantive pushback — the report is not unblemished.**

## 3. Metabunk / Mick West — episode-level technical analysis

Mick West: software engineer (Tony Hawk series), founder of Metabunk, author of *Escaping
the Rabbit Hole*. **The most technically serious critic of the show's specific claims.**
All threads 403-blocked; URLs confirmed to exist.

### (a) The LiDAR "wormhole" (S4E11) — the strongest debunk in the file
West's explanation: the central black area was a **shadow/occlusion — a region the LiDAR
simply didn't reach.** The surrounding red data was **spurious returns**, circular
**because the drone was rotating**. Both the black gap and the red ring **changed when the
LiDAR angle was adjusted** — the signature of a scan-geometry artifact, not a physical
structure. **West replicated this: he moved a phone-based LiDAR scanner up and down his
garden and eliminated both the data void and the ring artifacts.**

**Strength: very strong.** A specific mechanism, plus successful replication producing the
same artifact from mundane equipment, plus a falsifiable prediction (artifact moves with
scan angle) reportedly borne out on the show's own data. **This is the model of a good debunk.**

### (b) The laser beam gaps
Proposed mechanism: a beam is only visible where there are **scattering particles**; gaps
correspond to **regions of lower particle density** — evening dew as air cools, or a
warm-air vent creating a less-misty pocket. Secondary candidates: **rolling-shutter
artifacts** (the laser not emitting during the split second the sensor scanned that row),
and outright filtering/editing.

**Strength: moderate.** Multiple competing mundane mechanisms is *epistemically good* (the
phenomenon is over-determined by ordinary physics), **but Metabunk did not converge on one
demonstrated cause the way it did on the LiDAR.** Flag as "plausible mundane explanations
exist," **not** "demonstrated."

### (c) S3E09 "UAP disappearing into thin air"
Resolution embedded in the thread title: **a satellite going behind a cloud / entering
Earth's shadow.** **Strength: strong** — a checkable astronomical claim.

### (d) West vs. Fugal, direct engagement
*Tales From the Rabbit Hole* Ep. 45, 29–30 May 2020, ~1h41m. West pressed Fugal on
**"science or showmanship."** The exchange is described as **"amicable and collegial from
beginning to end"** [B]. **This is the best single artifact for a fair adversarial
record** — the chief skeptic and the owner in extended direct dialogue.

Other West threads: S2E8 "trailing UFO" (12203); magnetic-deviation debunk (12524).

## 6. The Myers family / Garth Myers — THE STRONGEST EMPIRICAL CRITIQUE

**The most damaging single line of evidence in the file.**

**The claim [A, corroborated across multiple independent results]:** the family that owned
the property **immediately before the Shermans lived there for over 60 years and reported
nothing anomalous whatsoever.**

- **Garth Myers** — an **M.D. in pediatric neurology**, career largely at LDS Primary
  Children's Hospital, also with the Utah State Department of Health — **denied any UFO or
  paranormal activity during his family's ownership.**
- Myers told **Frank Salisbury** — the USU botanist who wrote the *sympathetic* *Utah UFO
  Display* — that he had been close to his brother and sister-in-law who lived at the
  ranch, and **nothing strange had happened.**
- Source: "SKINWALKER RANCH: Original Owner/Family Member Sets The Record Straight," The
  UFO Chronicles, April 2020.

**Why this is so strong:** it is a **base-rate argument with a control period.** If the
location generates phenomena, 60 years of continuous residence should have produced
reports. It produced none. **The phenomena appear coincident with the Shermans' arrival
and their decision to go to the press.** Myers is a credentialed physician with no
financial or ideological stake, **and the testimony was given to a UFO-sympathetic
researcher, not a hostile one — which removes the "skeptic led the witness" objection.**

**Ranch-side rebuttal [B] — and it is weak:** Ryan Skinner has accused **both Myers and
Salisbury of a cover-up, "basing this on their adherence to the Mormon faith,"** citing
**Gwen Sherman's** testimony that Myers was being untruthful.

> **Flag as a bad argument.** Imputing dishonesty to two named individuals on the basis of
> their religion is an ad hominem, and the corroborating witness is the interested party
> whose family's account is the very thing in dispute. **This is circular. It does not
> neutralize the Myers testimony.**

## 9. Cattle mutilations — the best-settled question in the file

**Findings [A, multiply corroborated]:**
- **FBI / Kenneth Rommel investigation** (Operation Animal Mutilation, New Mexico, ~1980):
  examined 15 cases, concluded **all** were explicable by natural predation and
  decomposition.
- **Mechanism for "surgical" incisions:** postmortem **bloat stretches the hide until it
  tears**, and **dehydration shrinks and splits** it — producing clean linear openings
  that read as scalpel cuts to untrained observers.
- **Mechanism for missing soft tissue:** lips, tongue, eyes, genitals and anus are
  consumed **first** by blowflies, maggots, ravens and small scavengers, leaving edges
  that look excised.
- **Anthropologist Nancy Owen** concluded events were consistent with scavenger activity.
- **Experimental replication: the Washington County (Arkansas) Sheriff's Department** ran
  a controlled experiment in which a cow carcass developed **bloat-induced incision-like
  tears matching reported "surgical" cuts**, with blowfly/maggot damage matching reported
  soft-tissue loss.

**Strength: very strong — demonstrated, not asserted.** A proposed mechanism, a federal
investigation, and successful experimental replication. **Any ranch-side mutilation claim
must clear this bar first, and the burden is squarely on the claimant.**

## 10. The Navajo / Diné appropriation question

**The geographic argument — decisive [A]:** the ranch **is not on Navajo land.** It sits
near the **Uintah and Ouray Reservation — Ute territory.** **Skinwalkers do not feature in
Ute religion.** The name imports a Diné concept into a place with no Diné connection.

**The semantic argument [A]:** a *yee naaldlooshii* is **not a cryptid or monster
species.** It is a **human being** associated with harmful witchcraft who may take,
possess, or disguise themselves as an animal. The TV/tourism usage — a lurking creature
hunted with instruments — **is a category error, not merely a borrowing.**

**The taboo argument [A]:** the tradition is **rarely discussed with outsiders at all**;
many Diné will not say the word. **Commercializing it is objectionable not only because it
distorts, but because the discussion itself violates the practice.**

**Was the name attached before the 1990s? Evidence says NO [A/B].** The property was known
as **the Sherman Ranch** and was **renamed** afterwards; the modern association "appears to
be a **recent development shaped by retelling and tourism**." This dovetails with
Colavito's "entirely a modern creation" and Kloor's "media-manufactured myth."

> **Combined: "Skinwalker Ranch" is a post-1990s marketing overlay applying a Navajo
> concept to a Ute-adjacent property previously known as the Sherman Ranch.** Rated
> **B+/A-** — the pieces are well-attested but no source document confirming the exact
> renaming date or agent could be read.

**Documented Native pushback (nearest analogue) [A]:** *Indian Country Today* on the
backlash to the *Ghost Adventures* skinwalker episode — including a Navajo citizen who said
it was **shameful that the Navajo Nation permitted "such a misinformed, exploitive
depiction of Dine' culture without providing some cultural guidance."**

**Honest gap:** **no named Diné scholar on record about *Skinwalker Ranch specifically***
(as opposed to skinwalker media generally) was found. **Do not fabricate one.**

## 14. COUNTER-POINT: the strongest good-faith defenses

**(a) The Uintah Basin sighting cluster predates the ranch mythology — the best defense
there is.** **Frank B. Salisbury** was a *bona fide* scientist — professor of plant
physiology at Utah State. With **Junior Hicks** he compiled **400+ first-hand sighting
accounts dating to the 1950s**, published as *The Utah UFO Display: A Scientist's Report*
(1974). Salisbury laid out competing hypotheses explicitly, **including the debunkers'
position** — he argued in good faith rather than assuming his conclusion.

> **This is a real defense and it should be granted: the *regional* sighting cluster is
> documented, decades older than the ranch story, and catalogued by a credentialed
> scientist.** But note the crucial disjunction — **this same Salisbury is the researcher
> to whom Garth Myers said nothing ever happened at the ranch.** So Salisbury's own corpus
> supports "the Basin has an unexplained sighting record" **while simultaneously
> undercutting "*this particular 512 acres* is special." That distinction is the crux of
> the whole project.**

**(b) The classified-data argument.** Genuinely valid in logical form: AAWSAP produced
**100+ technical reports delivered to DIA**, many still not public. Absence of public data
is therefore **not dispositive.** Counterweight: **AARO — which had the clearances and the
access — reviewed the record and reported claimants supplied no empirical evidence.** The
argument survives in principle but is weakened by the one body positioned to test it.

**(c) Credentialed scientists lend some weight.** **Kevin Knuth** — Associate/full
Professor of Physics, SUNY Albany; former NASA Ames; **Editor-in-Chief of *Entropy***.
Reviewed S1E1: "It appears that science has overlooked a great deal in terms of strange,
rare, and uncontrollable phenomena."

> **Crucially — and this is a mark of Knuth's integrity — he also stated plainly that the
> show is "a TV show," "not a documentary," and that some scenes appear re-enacted or
> staged [A]. Use this.** The most scientifically credible defender of the *subject matter*
> is on record disclaiming the *program.* **That is the fairest possible framing of the
> Knuth data point: it cuts against the show while supporting continued inquiry.**

**Garry Nolan** — **no evidence he has endorsed Skinwalker Ranch specifically**; the
association is largely by adjacency. **Do not overstate his involvement.**

**Erik Bard** — profiled in **Popular Mechanics (2024)** as a *former skeptic* converted by
a 2016 visit (phone screen flickering near the mesa; balance disturbance). **Weak as
evidence** — a flickering phone and dizziness are exactly the class of observation most
vulnerable to expectation effects, and the conversion narrative is a standard trope. **But
it is a sincere datapoint from a credentialed person, and fairness requires including it.**

**(d) The BAASS internal framing was not purely credulous.** A 2009 BAASS report described
the ranch as a "**possible laboratory for studying other intelligences and possible
interdimensional phenomena**" — framed as a *research site*, not a proven anomaly.

**(e) Errors in the AARO report are real** (Mellon and Powell) — the skeptical case should
not treat AARO as inerrant.

## Bottom line

**Strongest critiques, ranked:**
1. **The Myers family's 60-year null control period**, given to a *sympathetic* researcher.
2. **The cattle-mutilation literature** — experimentally demonstrated; forecloses an entire evidence category.
3. **Mick West's LiDAR replication** — the only debunk that reproduces the anomaly from mundane equipment.
4. **The DoD's explicit disclaimer** that clearing *Skinwalkers at the Pentagon* implies no endorsement of factual accuracy.
5. **The structural critique** — no public data, no replication, no peer review, seven seasons without resolution.

regional sighting record is real, predates the ranch mythology by forty years, and was
catalogued by a credentialed scientist.**

> **The decisive move is to hold apart *the Basin* (defensible) from *the 512 acres* (where
